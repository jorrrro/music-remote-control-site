Music Remote Control

Music Remote Control lets you control Music on your Mac from your iPhone, iPad, widgets, Live Activities, and Shortcuts on Mac. You can also browse your library, search Apple Music, view playlists and stations, and manage Up Next remotely.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to run the Mac app?

Yes. Music Remote Control must be running on your Mac whenever you use the iPhone or iPad app, Home Screen widget, Live Activity, or remote browsing features. The Mac app’s window does not need to remain visible; it can continue running in the menu bar.

How do the different apps work?

  • Mac app (macOS 15+): Controls Music, provides library and Apple Music content to connected devices, manages Up Next, and shares playback information.
  • iPhone and iPad app (iOS 16+): Controls playback, browses your library and playlists, searches Apple Music, displays recent stations, and manages Up Next on the Mac.
  • Home Screen widget (iOS 17+): Provides quick Previous, Play/Pause, Next, and Favorite controls. The medium widget also provides Suggest Less.
  • Live Activity (iOS 17+): Provides Previous, Play/Pause, Next, Favorite, and Suggest Less controls on supported Lock Screens and Dynamic Island, as well as Apple Watch views on iOS 18+ and watchOS 11+.

How do I connect my mobile device?

  1. Open Music Remote Control on the Mac.
  2. Open Music Remote on the iPhone or iPad.
  3. Allow Local Network access when requested.
  4. Keep both devices nearby with Wi-Fi and Bluetooth enabled.
  5. Wait for them to connect automatically. No pairing code is required.

What can I control?

The available features include:

  • Previous, Play/Pause, and Next
  • Song-position seeking and Apple Music volume
  • Favorite, Remove Favorite, and Suggest Less
  • Shuffle, Repeat Off, Repeat All, and Repeat One
  • AirPlay output selection and individual device volume
  • Browse and play songs, artists, and albums from your library
  • Search for and play songs from the Apple Music catalog
  • Browse playlists and play individual playlist songs
  • Play recently used Apple Music stations
  • Add, remove, play, and clear songs in Up Next

What can I browse from my iPhone or iPad?

  • Library: Browse songs, artists, and albums from your Mac’s Music library. You can play songs, shuffle the library, or add songs to Up Next.
  • Search: Search the Apple Music catalog and play a result or add it to Up Next.
  • Playlists: View your playlists and the songs inside them.
  • Stations: View and play recently used Apple Music stations.
  • Up Next: View and manage the queue shared by every iPhone or iPad connected to the same Mac.

How do I view and play songs in a playlist?

  1. Open the Playlists tab on your iPhone or iPad.
  2. Select a playlist to view its songs.
  3. Search the playlist or sort it by playlist order, title, artist, album, or genre.
  4. Tap the Play button beside a song to play it, or tap the plus button to add it to Up Next.

Use Play Playlist in the playlist toolbar to play the entire playlist.

How does playlist caching work?

In the Playlists tab, tap Cache at the top right and enable Cache Playlists. This saves playlist names, folder structure, and song lists from playlists you open on this device for faster loading when you reopen the app.

Artwork loads separately and is not saved to the cache. Caching does not download music for offline playback.

After changing playlists or folders in Music on your Mac, pull down on the playlist list to update the saved information. To update the songs inside a playlist, open that playlist and pull down.

How does library caching work?

In the Library tab, tap Cache at the top right and enable Cache Library. This saves your library’s song information on this device for faster loading.

After changing your library in Music on your Mac, pull down on the Library page to update the saved information.

How does Up Next work?

Tap the plus button beside a song in Search, Library, or a playlist to append it to Up Next. Repeated songs are allowed.

If a song is already playing, Music Remote Control waits for it to finish before starting Up Next. You can also open the Up Next tab and select Play to start the queue immediately.

Up Next uses a Music playlist named Remote Up Next. Removing an entry or clearing the queue only removes it from this playlist. It does not delete the song from your Music library.

Why didn’t Up Next start automatically?

If you manually play a different song after adding songs to Up Next, Music Remote Control treats that as an intentional selection and cancels the automatic handoff. Your queued songs remain available in the Up Next tab, where you can select Play to start them.

If nothing was playing when you added the songs, open Up Next and select Play. The Mac app must remain running to detect when the current song finishes and start the queue automatically.

How do I use Music Remote Control with Shortcuts on Mac?

  1. Open Music Remote Control on your Mac at least once.
  2. Open the Shortcuts app on your Mac and create a new shortcut.
  3. Search for Remote in the action picker.
  4. Choose Previous Track (Remote), Play or Pause (Remote), Next Track (Remote), Toggle Favorite (Remote), or Suggest Less (Remote).
  5. Run the shortcut to control Music on your Mac.

You can link these shortcuts to a keyboard, controller, macro pad, smart-home switch, or another physical device if it can trigger Shortcuts on your Mac. This lets you use a physical device to control Music on your Mac.

For reliable operation, keep Music Remote Control running in the Mac menu bar.

  1. On your Mac, right-click the Music Remote Control mini player and select Home Automation. If you’re using Menu Bar Mode, click the song title in the macOS menu bar and select Home Automation.
  2. Copy the URL for the command you want.
  3. In the Home app, create an automation and choose its trigger.
  4. On the accessories and scenes screen, scroll down and select Convert to Shortcut.
  5. Add Get Contents of URL, paste the copied URL, and change the method to POST.
  6. Test the automation, then tap Done.

Your Home hub sends the command to your Mac over the local network. Your Mac must be awake with Music Remote Control running, and your Home hub must be on the same network.

Why doesn’t Suggest Less appear immediately in Music.app?

Music.app may continue showing a cached value for the currently playing song after Music Remote Control successfully applies Suggest Less. If the song is in your library, right-click it there. If the menu shows Undo Suggest Less, the rating was applied correctly. After you quit and reopen Music.app, its player interface should show the updated value.

What is Window Mode on Mac?

Window Mode displays the Mac mini player with artwork, song information, playback progress, transport controls, volume, and AirPlay controls. Right-click the mini player to open the app menu.

What does Pin do?

Pin keeps the Mac mini player above windows. The setting is remembered the next time the app opens.

What is Menu Bar Mode?

Menu Bar Mode hides the mini-player window and places compact Previous, Play/Pause, Next, and Favorite controls in the macOS menu bar. The current song title is also displayed there.

Click the song title and choose Switch to Window Mode to restore the mini player.

Why isn’t the Mac app in the Dock?

Music Remote Control is designed as a menu-bar utility. Look for its icon or the current song title in the macOS menu bar.

What do the connection icons mean on iPhone/iPad?

  • White spinner: Searching for or connecting to the Mac.
  • Green Mac and iPhone: Connected and ready.
  • Orange question mark: Connected, but the trial needs to be started or the full version needs to be unlocked on the Mac.
  • Red Mac: Disconnected or unable to connect.

Tap the status icon to display a written status message.

Why is the artwork missing?

Apple Music does not always make artwork available to other apps. Artwork may be unavailable when listening to radio-style content such as Discovery Station, personalized stations, or some live stations.

This is an Apple Music API limitation and does not mean the connection has failed. Playback controls and song information will continue to work normally.

How do I add the Home Screen widget on iOS?

  1. Touch and hold an empty area of the Home Screen.
  2. Choose Edit or the Add Widget button.
  3. Search for Music Remote.
  4. Select the small or medium widget.
  5. Add it to the Home Screen.

Does the mobile app need to be open for the widget?

The mobile app does not need to remain visible. However, the Music Remote Control Mac app must be running.

What does the Live Activity provide?

After the mobile app connects to the Mac, the Live Activity provides Previous, Play/Pause, Next, and Favorite controls.

Apple Watch support is provided through the iPhone Live Activity. It is not a separate Watch app.

Why doesn’t the Live Activity appear?

  • Confirm that the mobile app is connected to the Mac.
  • Confirm that the Mac app is running.
  • Enable Live Activities for Music Remote in iOS/watchOS Settings.
  • Confirm that the trial or Full Unlock is active.

What does the AirPlay screen control?

It controls AirPlay destinations available to Apple Music on the Mac. It does not control the iPhone or iPad system audio output.

A checkmark indicates a selected destination. A lock indicates that the destination is protected. Individual volume controls appear for selected AirPlay outputs.

Why is an AirPlay device missing?

  1. Confirm that the device appears in Apple Music on the Mac.
  2. Open the AirPlay screen and tap Refresh.
  3. Confirm that the Mac and AirPlay device are on a compatible network.
  4. Quit and reopen Apple Music if its AirPlay list appears stale.

How does the free trial work?

The Mac app provides a free 14-day trial of all remote-control features. Starting the trial does not create an automatic charge.

When the trial ends, purchase the one-time Full Unlock to continue using the remote features. There is no subscription.

Where do I purchase or restore access?

Trial, purchase, and restoration options are available in the Mac app. Open its menu and select the purchase-status item. Use Restore Purchases if you previously purchased Full Unlock with the same Apple Account.

The mobile app cannot find my Mac

  • Make sure Music Remote Control is running on the Mac.
  • Open the Mac app before opening the mobile app.
  • Keep the devices nearby.
  • Enable Wi-Fi and Bluetooth.
  • Allow Local Network access.
  • Confirm that the macOS firewall allows incoming connections for Music Remote Control.

The connection remains stuck

Restart both Music Remote apps in this order:

  1. On iPhone or iPad, open the App Switcher and swipe Music Remote away.
  2. On Mac, open the Music Remote Control menu and choose Quit Music Remote Control.
  3. Reopen Music Remote Control on the Mac first.
  4. Wait a few seconds.
  5. Reopen Music Remote on the iPhone or iPad.

The apps connect, but the controls don’t work

  • Confirm that Apple Music is open on the Mac.
  • Confirm that the trial or Full Unlock is active.
  • On the Mac, open System Settings → Privacy & Security → Automation and allow Music Remote Control to control Music.
  • Confirm that the mobile connection icon is green rather than orange.

The widget does nothing

Confirm that the Mac app is running, the devices are nearby, Local Network access is enabled, Apple Music is available, and remote access is active. The widget may take several seconds to discover the Mac and may not display an error if the Mac cannot be reached.
